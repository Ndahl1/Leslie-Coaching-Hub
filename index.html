The 7 steps — tap each one the consultant lands

Tap once = landed clean. Twice = missed or weak (becomes the coaching note). Again to clear.

Qualify before you answer

Ask it"Tell me about the install — any issues I should know about?"
Ask it"What's the price difference from what you quoted?"
Enter both numbers — I'll show the gap and a counter.
Grant"[Name], you've been doing this a long time and I respect your opinion. If you truly feel it's easier than normal and the company would be okay at that price, go take care of her — and tell her thank you for me."
Counter"…what I like most about you is you always want to help the customer. But you've been doing this a long time — if you priced it at $[quote], it's a $[quote] job. I'll work with them and do $[counter]. Go take care of her, and tell her thank you for me."

The brief — five parts, tap what you got

Anything not green before you take the customer — ask for it. Especially #5.

Run the Wheel

1 · Open warm"Mr. and Mrs. ___, this is Nels, the sales manager at Leslie. I never let a consultant leave without checking in personally — did we take good care of you today?"
2 · Acknowledge · Empathize · By-Pass"I can absolutely understand that. If I were in your shoes I'd feel the same way. Let's suppose you did make a decision today — which option would you choose?"
Isolate the logical"My job is to inform you so you can make the best decision for your situation. If you don't mind me asking — what questions are you still thinking about?"
50 / 40 / 10"Half is materials and labor. About 40% is what it takes to be in business — the trucks, the training, the people who answer at 2 AM. The last 10% is what we keep if everything goes perfect. Can you see how our pricing is transparent and fair? Should we go ahead?"
Inverted wheel"Every project is Time, People, Company, and System. The System never changes — same equipment. So the only way to cut the price is to shrink the circle. What distinguishing factor lets them charge less for this project?"
Next Availability Install Program"When we have a gap in the install schedule, filling it earns you a discount. What would the number have to be?" (No number → step down $200–300 at a time.)
5-year math — go slow, write it down"Over five years: the repairs you'll keep making, plus what the old system overpays in energy, plus the replacement you'll end up buying anyway — against replacing today under full warranty. Can you see how it actually saves you to replace today?"
Like / dislike → suppose"What did you like about option B? What do you think [spouse] would say? Suppose [spouse] is on board — which option?" Then: call them now → swing back tonight → book the second appointment.
Home inspector story"Any system over 10–12 years gets flagged in the inspection, and buyers negotiate ten, twelve thousand off. You're buying the system either way — the only question is whether you get to use it."
4 · The ask — a question, not a statement"Should we go ahead and provide this service for you then?"
Still no — protect & date it"Completely understand — you two talk it over. I'm going to have [consultant] check back with you Thursday evening — does that work? And the quote is locked until then."

Close the loop — 30 seconds, ONE note

Where the numbers come from

    
There is exactly one path. Nels logs a day; the hub sums the days into a month; every scoreboard in the tool reads that month. Nothing is typed twice, and no two screens can disagree.

    
  


  
  

    
How the coaching loop closes

    
The second loop runs on what consultants log, and it has one job: no unsold appointment leaves the week without a documented fight, and no commitment goes ungraded.

    
  


  
  

    
Signing in, PINs and the month box

    
The hub opens on a shared link — no account, no password. Three controls in the header govern everything below them.

    
    
      
  1. Your PIN. Pick your name in the Consultant dropdown; the hub asks for your PIN the first time you open your own touch base or track record. It holds for that browser session. PINs aren't printed anywhere — ask Nels if you've lost yours. They exist so somebody else's honest self-score doesn't get saved under your name on a shared office tablet.
    2. 
      
  2. The month box. One picker drives Scoreboard, Daily Log, Team, Forecast and Year over Year together. It does not drive the coaching tabs — those follow their own week pickers, so last week's touch bases can be reviewed against this month's numbers.
    3. 
      
  3. The sync dot. Green means the Google Sheet has everything. Red means the hub is working from the copy on this device and will push when the connection returns — keep going, nothing is lost.
    4. 
      
  4. Manager. Press it, enter the manager PIN, and it turns red. That unlocks Daily Log, Import, Pace & Setup and Call Coach, adds the notes boxes to the Monday board, and opens any consultant's form without their PIN. It lasts for the browser session only, which is what you want on a shared machine. The end-of-day team rollup has its own separate PIN so a borrowed laptop can't send it.
    5. 
    

  


  
  

    
My Touch Base Consultant

    
Before Monday · about 10 minutes · saving again for the same week updates it

    
You bring the plan; Nels brings the questions. Ten minutes of prep buys a conversation about your process instead of a conversation about your numbers.

    
    
      
  1. Pick your name and the week. The date box snaps to Monday whatever day you click, so you can't file against the wrong week.
    2. 
      
  2. Read your scoreboard — section 1. Appointments run, both close rates, both average tickets, each with year-to-date underneath and a flag against target. You type none of it. The line above the tiles tells you whether it came from the daily log or the monthly pace report.
    3. 
      
  3. Grade last week — section 2. Last week's plan is quoted back with three buttons: Worked, Partially, Didn't work. Then say what you actually saw — which calls, what the customer did. This is the field the Track Record is built from; a blank one means the week never gets graded.
    4. 
      
  4. Self-check — section 3. Rate all six steps 1–5 for last week's calls. A 5 means you ran that step the way we train it on every call. The hub flags your lowest step as a candidate for this week's focus.
    5. 
      
  5. Wins — section 4. What worked and specifically what you did to make it work. "I built rapport" isn't a win; "I restated her concern about the upstairs bedroom before I presented" is. The second box is for one thing you did to get better — that counts even in a week the numbers didn't.
    6. 
      
  6. Opportunity — section 5. Pick the KPI to move (sub-standard ones sort to the top) and the step it lives in, then name the cause in process terms. "Not enough appointments" is a circumstance; "I'm not attaching to anything they said out loud" is a cause you can act on.
    7. 
      
  7. Action plan — section 6. Four boxes, all four answerable on Wednesday:
        
      
          
    • Behavior — one thing, observable on a ride-along.
      • 
          
    • When — the trigger moment in the call, not "throughout".
      • 
          
    • Measure — a count. "My ticket will be higher" can't be counted; "X of Y quotes carried an attachment" can.
      • 
          
    • Checkpoint — a day and a time.
      • 
        
    
      
    8. 
      
  8. What you need from Nels — section 7. Roleplay time, pricing help, a ride-along, an answer from leadership. It lands on the Monday board in a flagged box.
    9. 
      
  9. Save my touch base. You can start Friday and finish Sunday — saving again updates the same entry.
    10.

Whatever you write in Behavior becomes the focus card on your Debrief tab, and every call you log afterwards asks whether you ran it. Write it as something you either did or didn't do on a given call and the tally works. Write it as an aspiration and there's nothing to count.

Review & agree

    
After the one-on-one, Nels writes his notes and what you practiced live onto your entry, and a card appears at the bottom of your touch base with both plus an agree button. Read them, press agree, and a green tick shows on your Monday card. If Nels edits the notes afterwards the tick clears and you'll be asked to read the new version — so you never end up on record agreeing to something you didn't read.

  


  
  

    
Debrief Consultant

    
After every appointment · about 60 seconds · in the truck, before you drive away

    
This is the one that matters. Everything else in the hub is built out of what gets logged here, and it has to happen while the call is still fresh. An hour later you'll remember the outcome and nothing else.

    
    
      
  1. Check the focus card. If you filed a touch base this week your focus behavior sits at the top in quotes with a running tally — ran it on 3 of 5 calls. That's the number Nels asks about on Wednesday.
    2. 
      
  2. Customer. A first name is plenty. It's what shows in the coverage queue if the call didn't sell, so make it something you'd recognize at 6pm.
    3. 
      
  3. How did it end? Sold, Pending, or No sale. Pending means a real dated next step exists, not "they're thinking about it".
    4. 
      
  4. Did you run your focus behavior? Only appears if you have a plan this week. Answer honestly — a "skipped it" that shows a pattern is worth more than a padded count.
    5. 
      
  5. Restate-to-nod. Did you say their top concern back in their words and get the nod before you opened the tablet? Every call, whatever your focus is.
    6. 
      
  6. One thing they said — their exact words. Not a summary. "The upstairs bedroom is an oven by 3pm." That line opens your next presentation and it's the line Nels reads back on Monday.
    7. 
      
  7. If it sold: the amount and the option they chose. If it didn't: the High Authority / Zero Ticket call — Made, Scheduled or Not yet — who you called, and what's holding them back in their words.
    8. 
      
  8. Save this call. The form clears and your week's tally updates underneath.
    9. 
    

    

      The one that costs money
      
An unsold call logged as Not yet lands in Nels's coverage queue immediately and stays there until someone makes the call. That's the point — an unsold appointment nobody fought for is the most expensive thing in your week. Log it as Not yet rather than skipping the debrief; the queue is help, not a black mark.

    

    
The Wednesday checkpoint

    
At the bottom of the same tab: Text Nels my checkpoint. It builds the message from what you already logged — focus count, restate-to-nod, calls, sold, HA/ZT coverage — and hands it to your phone. You don't type a checkpoint; you press a button.

    
The end-of-day team rollup Nels only

    
A card at the bottom of the same tab, gated by the end-of-day PIN, covering today only across the whole team: calls, sold and revenue, pending, no-sale, restate-to-nod, HA/ZT coverage with anything still open named, then a short block per consultant with each call's customer words and holdback — and finally who logged nothing today. That last line is the useful one; a consultant who ran four appointments and logged none is invisible everywhere else.

Team Review Everyone can read it Notes are Nels only

    
Monday · the board the one-on-ones are run from

    
Everyone's week on one screen — numbers, self-check, wins, opportunity, plan and asks for the selected week. It's open to the team on purpose: the team's numbers aren't a secret from the team, and seeing how somebody else framed their opportunity is the fastest way to write a better one.

    
    
      
  1. Set the week. The date snaps to Monday. Anyone who hasn't filed shows an empty card — which is itself the first conversation.
    2. 
      
  2. Read the card top down. KPI chips flagged against target, the six-step self-check as a meter, last week's result with the note, wins, the field block, growth, the opportunity, this week's plan, and the asks in a flagged box.
    3. 
      
  3. Check the field block against the plan. From the field shows calls, sold, focus hits out of focus-eligible calls, restate-to-nod, and HA/ZT coverage on unsold, plus any coached calls. A self-check of 5 next to a focus count of 1 of 6 is the conversation.
    4. 
      
  4. Write the notes. Two boxes — manager notes, and what was actually run live and how it landed — then Save manager fields.
    5. 
      
  5. Watch for the agreed tick. The consultant reads both boxes on their own touch base and presses agree. Editing the notes afterwards clears the tick and asks them to read it again.
    6. 
    

    

      The asks are the deal
      
The needs box is the consultant's fastest lane to you, and it only stays credible if it gets answered. Anything committed to on Monday, report on next Monday — the board is the record either way.

    

  


  
  

    
Track Record Consultant

    
Whenever you want the honest view · PIN-protected per consultant

    
History by month: what the KPIs did, what was committed each week, whether it worked, and whether the number actually moved. Nobody types this — it assembles itself from touch bases and the daily log.

    
    
      
  • Month rows — the five KPIs flagged against target. Click to expand the weeks inside.
    • 
      
  • Commitment — the behavior named that week.
    • 
      
  • Result — how it was graded the following week: Worked, Partial, Didn't, or Pending if it was never graded.
    • 
      
  • Moved — whether the named KPI shifted between the month of the commitment and four weeks on, with the before and after on hover.
    • 
      
  • Export CSV — the whole table, for a review or your own records.
    • 
    

    

      The only way this gets thin
      
Every "Pending" is a week where the plan was never graded. Grading takes one button in section 2 of the next Monday's touch base. Skip it a few weeks running and the Track Record stops being able to tell you anything.

    

  


  
  

    
Call Coach Manager only

    
Live, while the phone is ringing

    
The High Authority and Zero Ticket playbook, built for the moment you pick up. Everything you tap becomes a coached-call record on that consultant's week.

    
The coverage queue

    
At the top: every unsold appointment from this week and last week with no HA/ZT call made, newest first, with the customer, the consultant and the holdback in their words. Press Start this call and the hub sets the consultant, customer and brief note for you and opens in Zero Ticket mode. When you end the call, that debrief is marked covered and drops off the queue. Scheduled keeps it in the queue on purpose — only made clears it.

    
Running a call

    
    
      
  1. Pick the mode. High Authority — the consultant is the voice, you're behind the curtain. Zero Ticket — you take the customer.
    2. 
      
  2. Pick the consultant and customer and press Answer the call. A timer starts.
    3. 
      
  3. Tap as you go. Each checklist item cycles: once for landed clean, twice for missed or weak, again to clear. The misses become the coaching note if you don't write one.
    4. 
      
  4. High Authority: the seven steps plus frame held, with the qualifying questions and the quote-versus-ask calculator that gives you the counter number.
    5. 
      
  5. Zero Ticket: the five-part brief first — anything not green, ask for it before you take the customer, especially what they think the real objection is. Then the wheel: open warm, acknowledge and empathize and by-pass, name the spoke and run its play, ask for the business, set a dated next step.
    6. 
      
  6. Record the outcome, write the one coaching note, and press End call. Copy the record into Teams or ServiceTitan if you want it there too — it's already saved to the consultant's week.
    7. 
    

    

      One note, not five
      
The note box is deliberately singular. A consultant who gets one specific correction after a coached call changes one thing; a consultant who gets five changes nothing.

The lead plan Everyone can read it

    
One set of numbers · read by five tabs

    
Revenue is the outcome. Leads run is the input you can actually manage day to day, so the hub carries a monthly lead plan alongside the revenue target and measures every day against it.

    

      
NumberWhat it means

      
TGL leads — monthTech-generated appointments the team has to run this month.

      
Marketed leads — monthMarketed appointments for the month.

      
Total leadsTGL plus marketed. Override it only if your plan's total isn't the sum of the two.

      
Appointments a day, per consultantThe individual bar — what one consultant is expected to run on a working day.

    

    
Where the pace line comes from. The monthly plan is divided by the budgeted working days on the Forecast tab. That gives a per-day rate for each category. “Expected by now” is that rate times the number of days logged, and the green or red tag is what you've run minus what was expected. Anything inside half a lead reads as on pace.

    

      Set it once
      
All four numbers live in Forecast → Lead plan, per month. Change them there and the Scoreboard, Daily Log, Team and Year over Year tabs all move together — there is nowhere else to enter them, so they cannot disagree.

    

    

      
TabWhat it shows about leads

      
ScoreboardMonth-to-date leads by category against plan and pace, plus a plain-language ahead/behind line.

      
Daily LogLeads run on each individual day by category, and the running month-to-date position after that day.

      
TeamEach consultant's leads run against their own daily bar, and the team against the plan.

      
ForecastThe plan itself, leads needed per remaining day, and where the lead count lands at the current run rate.

      
Year over YearLeads run this year against the same days last year, with the plan alongside.

    

  


  
  

    
Daily Log Manager only

    
Every workday · about 2 minutes · the single source of truth

    
One card per calendar day. Days with data carry a green edge, weekends sit on a tinted ground, and each card shows that day's revenue, the team's service calls, and last year's figure for the same date.

    
Leads on each card. A logged day also shows how many leads were run that day split TGL / marketed, with a tag for that day against the daily pace, and a second line giving the month-to-date total against the plan with its own tag. Read down the grid and you can see exactly which days put you ahead or behind. The line at the top of the tab restates the plan and the daily rate it works out to.

    
    
      
  1. Check the month box first. Everything on this tab belongs to the month in the header.
    2. 
      
  2. Press Log today, or Edit on any day card.
    3. 
      
  3. Service calls. The team's total calls run that day. It feeds the Scoreboard tile and nothing else — leave it at zero if you don't track it.
    4. 
      
  4. Fill the six columns per consultant. TGL appointments, sold, revenue; then the same three for marketed. Revenue is the sold total for that lead type that day, not an average.
    5. 
      
  5. Watch the warning line. It flags sold exceeding appointments, or revenue booked with no sale, as you type. Those are the two mistakes that quietly corrupt a close rate.
    6. 
      
  6. Save day. It writes to the Google Sheet immediately and every other tab recalculates. Leave a consultant's row blank if they didn't run — a blank is different from a zero, and blanks keep them out of the day's averages.
    7.

Open the day, change the number, save. There is nothing else to update: the month total, every consultant's touch-base scoreboard, the Monday board chips, the Track Record and the year-to-date all re-derive from the day you just fixed.

Team Everyone can read it Roster edits are Nels only

    
The month arranged for comparison

    
Appointments, sold and close rate split by lead type, then total sold, revenue, average sale and overall close. Underneath: leads run against the daily bar, a close-rate matrix and each person's share of the month's revenue.

    
Leads run vs the daily bar. Every consultant is expected to run the same number of appointments a day — the bar set on the Forecast tab. This table shows what each has actually run, what the bar says they should have by now, and the gap. It is a volume conversation, not a conversion one: someone at 100% of the bar with a 30% close rate has a different problem from someone at 60% of the bar closing well.

    
    
      
  1. Add a consultant. Type the name as you want it shown everywhere — "Jeremy M." style keeps the tables narrow. They appear immediately in every dropdown, the day editor and the Monday board.
    2. 
      
  2. Rename one. This is a real migration, not a label change: the hub moves their PIN, every logged day, every monthly pace row and every touch base to the new name, then re-syncs all of it. Use it for a spelling fix — not to hand a seat to a new hire.
    3. 
      
  3. Someone leaves. Leave them on the roster until the year is out so their history stays readable. They'll show zeros in the current month, which is accurate.

Forecast Everyone can read it

    
Assumptions are set per month

    
Where the month lands if the current appointment pace holds. Three scenarios, all built from what's actually been logged rather than from a guess.

    
    
      
  1. Set the month's assumptions in the panel at the bottom: revenue target, working days, and fallback average sales. Working days default to the weekdays in the month — knock off holidays by hand.
    2. 
      
  2. Set the month's lead plan in the panel below that: TGL leads, marketed leads, and each consultant's daily appointment bar. Working days divides the plan into the daily pace, so fix that number first. Full detail in The lead plan.
    3. 
      
  3. Read the tiles. Target, days logged, days remaining, revenue to date, and the daily revenue needed to land on target.
    4. 
      
  4. Read the scenarios. Base case runs the current close rate and average sale forward across the remaining days. Best case adds 20% to the close rate (capped at 70%) and 5% to the ticket; worst case takes 15% off the close rate and 5% off the ticket.
    5. 
    

    

      Read it honestly
      
The projection multiplies appointments-per-logged-day by the days remaining. Early in a month two logged days is a thin base and the numbers swing hard; it settles by the second week. Assumptions save per month, so setting September's target doesn't disturb August's history.

    

  


  
  

    
Year over Year Everyone can read it

    
Needs a prior-year baseline for the month you're viewing

    
This month against the same month last year, matched period for period — the comparison runs only through the latest day logged, so a half-logged month is never compared against a full one.

    
    
      
  • Lead tiles — TGL, marketed and total appointments run through the latest day logged, each against the same days last year and against this month's plan.
    • 
      
  • Tiles — revenue, both close rates, overall close and both average sales, each with last year underneath and the gap in points or percent.
    • 
      
  • Day table — last year's revenue and this year's, the daily difference, both running month-to-date totals, and the percentage gap.
    • 
      
  • Close rate & ticket by day — TGL and marketed side by side across both years. Read the TOTAL row; single days swing on one deal.
    • 
      
  • H1 A/B — the fixed January–June comparison. It doesn't change with the month selector.
    • 
    

    
August 2025 is loaded. Any month without a baseline says so plainly at the top and leaves the day tables empty rather than implying a comparison it can't make.

  


  
  

    
Pace & Setup Manager only

    
Monthly, and whenever the team changes

    
Four panels. The first is the fallback path for months without a daily log; the other three are the hub's settings.

    
Monthly pace report

    
Pick any date in the month and enter each consultant's raw counts, plus an optional all-leads total that captures self-gen and other lead types. Close rates and tickets are calculated for you, previewed live, and warned about if sold exceeds appointments.

    
This panel does not overwrite the daily log. If a month has logged days, those win and this grid is only the backup copy. Use it for history — months before the daily log existed — or a month where the log got away from you. Rates only mode exists for old months where the underlying counts are gone; any year containing a rates-only month reports its year-to-date as an estimate rather than pretending to be exact.

    
Import from the pace report

    
Paste the Sales Dispatch Order sheet — the header row starting Advisor's Name plus the consultant rows. Columns are read by name, so order doesn't matter. Nothing is written until you read the preview and press Apply, and nothing syncs until you then press Save pace data. A multi-month backfill file can be loaded here too.

    
Targets

    
The five numbers every flag in the hub triggers off. On pace is at or above target, Watch is within 85%, Sub-standard is below that. The appointments target is read as a monthly figure, so a year-to-date total is judged against target × months elapsed rather than the raw number.

    
PINs, team & connection

    
    
      
  • Consultant PINs — one each; blank removes the gate for that person. Manager mode bypasses all of them.
    • 
      
  • Consultants — the roster as a comma-separated list. The Team tab is the friendlier way to edit it.
    • 
      
  • Apps Script URL and team code — the connection to the Google Sheet. Blank runs local-only on one device.
    • 
      
  • Manager PIN and end-of-day PIN — both set here.

Import Manager only

    
Instead of typing a day · writes straight into the daily log

    
From the Nexstar Pace Report

    
    
      
  1. Open the month's pace report and go to the sheet for that date — the one named for the day, like 8th.
    2. 
      
  2. Scroll to the per-rep grid at the bottom, below Calls Ran.
    3. 
      
  3. Copy the consultant rows. Column order is fixed: name, TGL appointments, sold, revenue, then marketed appointments, sold, revenue. A header row is fine — it's skipped.
    4. 
      
  4. Set the date and the service-call count, paste, and press Import day.
    5. 
      
  5. Read the confirmation. It says how many consultants imported and names anyone whose row didn't match the roster.
    6. 
    

    
From ServiceTitan

    
    
      
  1. Reports → Sales → Technician Performance. Filter to the Sales business unit, date range set to that single day.
    2. 
      
  2. Expand the marketed and TGL columns and use Close Rate As Of.
    3. 
      
  3. Copy the rows including the header. This importer matches columns by name, so the header is what makes it work — and column order doesn't matter.
    4. 
      
  4. Set the date, paste, import. If it finds the header but no TGL or marketed columns it says so rather than importing zeros.
    5. 
    

    

      Name matching
      
Reports say "Jeremy Murray" or "Murray, Jeremy"; the hub says "Jeremy M." The importer matches on first name plus initial, either order. Anything it can't place is still imported under the name as typed and called out in the confirmation — so a new hire who isn't on the roster shows up as a stranger rather than silently vanishing. Add them on the Team tab and re-import that day.

Month rollover Manager only

    
Nothing breaks at midnight on the 1st — the hub is month-aware throughout. Five minutes of housekeeping keeps it clean.

    
    
      
  1. Change the month box to the new month. The Daily Log opens empty and waiting.
    2. 
      
  2. Set the Forecast assumptions — the revenue target from the new pace report, and working days minus holidays. Everything else carries forward.
    3. 
      
  3. Download a full backup from the Import tab for the month that just closed.
    4. 
      
  4. Check the closed month reconciles. Open the Scoreboard on the old month and compare against the pace report's monthly actuals. If they differ, a day is missing or double-entered — the Daily Log shows which.
    5. 
      
  5. Load the prior-year baseline for the new month if you want year-over-year.

When it looks wrong

    

      
What you seeWhat it means

      
Sync errorThe hub is working from the copy on this device and will push when the connection is back. Keep going — nothing is lost. If it stays red all day, tell Nels.

      
Scoreboard all dashesNo days logged for that month yet. It fills in as the month goes.

      
No focus card on DebriefNo touch base submitted this week. Debriefs still save and still count — you just won't be asked the focus question.

      
Numbers look offCheck the month box in the header first. Then tell Nels which day looks wrong — he can correct any single day and everything recalculates.

      
PIN not acceptedAsk Nels. Don't use somebody else's — the entry saves under whoever's name is in the dropdown.

      
Close rate above 100%Sold exceeds appointments on some day. The day editor warns as you type; open that consultant's days and find it.

      
Ticket looks wildRevenue logged against zero sold, or a day entered twice. Check the day cards for a duplicate figure.

      
A consultant reads zeroTheir name on the imported report didn't match the roster. Add them on the Team tab and re-import that day.

      
Touch base says "pace report"No days logged for that month, so it fell back. Log a day and it switches over.

      
Queue never emptiesUnsold debriefs stay until an HA/ZT is made. "Scheduled" keeps them there on purpose.

    

    
The short version. Debrief every call while you're still in the driveway. Write a behavior on Monday that can be counted. Grade last week before you plan this one. And log every day, in one place, on the day — every number in the hub is only ever as current as the last day logged.
